Auction result

Ranked by eCPM — bid × pCTR for a click campaign, bid × pCTR × pCVR for a conversion one. Second price: the winner pays what it needed to beat the next eCPM, divided back out by whatever it is charged for, so price < bid. “Matched clause” is the targeting expression that made the ad eligible, so every served ad can answer “why me”.

A cpc bid buys a click and prices against pCTR; an ocpm bid buys a conversion and prices against pCTR × pCVR. Two different units, comparable only once both become expected revenue per thousand impressions — which is the whole reason a conversion model exists.

Why filter inside the search

A global top-K holds roughly K × selectivity eligible ads, so “retrieve then filter” needs K to grow as 1/selectivity to hold recall. Filtering during traversal does not depend on K at all. Recall is measured against exact search restricted to the eligible set.

The ROC curve, and what its area means

Sort every test impression by predicted pCTR, highest first, then walk the list. A click steps the curve up by 1/clicks; a non-click steps it right by 1/non-clicks. The dashed diagonal is a coin flip. Area under the curve = the probability a random clicked impression outscores a random non-clicked one — because each rightward step sweeps a rectangle whose height is the share of clicks already above that non-click. The green curve is the generator's own probabilities: the best any model could do on this data.

GAUC — the question the ranker is actually asked

Pooled AUC is computed over every test row at once, so it gets credit for ordering impressions that are never ranked against each other — telling a high-CTR user from a low-CTR one is real signal and useless signal. GAUC computes AUC within each request and averages by impressions. It is almost always lower, and it is the number worth defending. Groups with no click contrast have no AUC and are dropped; the count is shown because a GAUC over a tenth of the traffic is a different claim.

Read against the ceiling, not against zero

The click simulator is a known function, so the AUC of its own probabilities is the best any model could score on this data. A bare AUC number cannot tell you whether 0.60 is good; the distance to the ceiling can.

pCVR — a second model, on a tenth of the data

Conversion is predicted only where a click happened, so it trains on a population this system's own ranker chose, and on far fewer rows. It reaches more of its ceiling than pCTR does and is less well calibrated — both follow from the same fact. Its positive rate is conversions per CLICK, so these numbers do not compare to the pCTR ones above.

Calibration

The auction prices with bid × pCTR, so a model that ranks perfectly and is scaled wrong misprices every impression. Ranking metrics cannot see this, which is why ECE sits next to AUC rather than under it. Points on the diagonal are calibrated.

The six decisions

  • Eligibility and relevance are different problems. The inverted index answers may this ad serve, and that must be exact — showing an untargeted ad is a billing and compliance failure, not a quality regression. ANN answers is it relevant, where approximate is fine and recall is a dial. The build verifies the index against brute-force predicate evaluation on sampled requests and reports mismatches; it is the one component with a correctness guard rather than a metric.
  • Boolean expression indexing, not a posting list per ad. Targeting is DNF: OR over conjunctions. The index maps (attribute, value) → (conjunction, predicate), and a conjunction fires when every one of its predicate bits is set. Counting predicates rather than postings is what keeps a user with four interests from over-satisfying a one-predicate clause.
  • Filter inside the ANN search. Measured, not assumed: at K=25 the global retrieve-then-filter path reaches 0.34 recall where filtered search reaches 0.91, and it only catches up at K=300 — thirty times the vectors scored for the same answer.
  • Calibration ranks alongside AUC. eCPM = bid × pCTR means the absolute value is the price, not just the order.
  • No parameter is per-ad. Ad identity enters through hashed buckets and through Beta-smoothed counting features, so a new ad needs a prior rather than a retrain. That is what makes the simple model the right one as the corpus grows; a per-ad embedding table would need the opposite.
  • Second-price auction, so the demo shows price separating from bid.

Built in 90 minutes — what that cost

Deliberately out of scope, in the order I would add them: pCVR and conversion objectives; budget pacing over time and frequency capping; a two-tower encoder trained with in-batch negatives to replace the hand-built ad vectors; GBDT or a small MLP to capture the interactions a linear model on hashed crosses cannot; real click logs (Criteo, Avazu, Taobao) instead of a simulator; counterfactual evaluation from logged propensities; and moving the index to a compressed posting-list format.

The model currently reaches about half of the available lift over random. The gap is interaction capacity, not features — the crosses that carry the signal are already hashed in, which is why a GBDT is the next thing to try rather than more feature engineering.

How the two indexes are built, today, in this demo

  • Offline, once, into the image. python -m app.build_index runs in the Dockerfile. It derives the corpus, both indexes and both models, and pickles them to /srv/artifacts/indexes.pkl. A container start loads that file — under a second — instead of deriving anything. Deriving at startup cost 48s idle and over three minutes on a busy machine, and was paid again on every restart.
  • Targeting index. Every ad's DNF targeting is flattened into posting lists keyed by (attribute, value), each entry holding conjunction × 8 + predicate. A request concatenates the lists for its own tokens, takes unique, and bincounts by conjunction — a clause fires when its hit count equals its arity. The unique is load-bearing: it counts distinct predicates, so four interests cannot over-satisfy a one-predicate clause four times over.
  • ANN index. k-means with 64 centroids over the ad vectors, then one inverted list per centroid. A query scores 64 centroids, probes the nearest nprobe, and scores exactly inside those. The same posting-list idea as targeting, keyed by geometry instead of attributes.
  • Not incremental, and that is the honest gap. An ad added after the build is invisible until the next one. Production layers a delta index merged periodically into the base, and an ANN that accepts inserts. The thing you must not index is the fast-moving state — budget, pacing, pause — which changes by the second and is checked at serve time instead.
  • The artifact refuses to be stale. It carries a signature over the scale knobs, the seed and a hash of adsys.py; a mismatch rebuilds. It is written to a temp path and renamed, so a half-written index can never be loaded. An index that loads cleanly and answers for a different corpus is worse than no index, because nothing tells you.

Five minutes, in order

What to open, what to say, and the question each point is really answering. The numbers are live — read them off the tabs rather than from here.

  • 1 · Open “Serve a request”, pick a profile. “One request, end to end, live in the container.” Point at the funnel: 30,000 ads → a few thousand eligible → 300 candidates → 5 served, with the milliseconds beside each. The claim: I can account for every stage and its cost.
  • 2 · Point at the asymmetry. Eligibility is exact and verified against brute force at build time; ANN is approximate with a measured recall curve. “Showing an ad outside its targeting is a billing and compliance failure. Showing a slightly less relevant ad is a quality cost. Those deserve different machinery, and conflating them is the mistake I was trying not to make.”
  • 3 · Show a matched clause in the auction table. Every served ad can answer why me — the exact targeting expression that admitted it, negations included. Advertisers and auditors both ask this.
  • 4 · Switch retrieval strategy to “search then filter”. Then go to Retrieval and show the K-sweep: at K=25 it reaches 0.34 recall where filtered search reaches 0.91, and only catches up at K=300 — thirty times the vectors scored for the same answer. “A global top-K holds about K × selectivity eligible ads, so this strategy needs K to grow as 1/selectivity. Filtering inside the search does not.”
  • 5 · Model tab: read AUC against the ceiling, never alone. The generator is a known function, so the AUC of its own probabilities is the best score anything could get. “0.58 means nothing; 0.58 of an available 0.67 is a statement.”
  • 6 · Then GAUC, and volunteer the limitation. Pooled AUC gets credit for ordering impressions that are never ranked against each other. GAUC asks the question the ranker is actually asked — within one request — and is lower. Offering the weaker, more honest number is the point.
  • 7 · Then calibration. “eCPM is bid × pCTR, so the probability is a price, not just an order. A model that ranks perfectly and is scaled 2× high overbids every impression, and no ranking metric can see it.” ECE sits beside AUC for that reason.
  • 8 · pCVR, and why it is a second model. A CPA bid buys a conversion and a CPC bid buys a click — different units, comparable only as expected revenue per thousand impressions. Show a mixed auction where an ad with a lower bid outranks a higher one.
  • 9 · Close on what you got wrong. The count leakage, the GBDT that lost anyway, the L2 that measured zero, the embedding scale bug. “Here is what I measured, here is what surprised me, here is what I still do not know” beats a clean story — and the follow-up questions land somewhere you have already been.

Four things that went wrong, and what each one taught

  • Counting features leaked the label. Each row's ad_ctr included its own click. Nothing in AUC says “leak” — it showed up as a model that could not be improved, with boosting stopping after 3 rounds. Fixed by time-shifting every row's counts to the history before it. It cost the linear model 0.5976 → 0.5918, and the lower number is the true one.
  • The GBDT lost, and the leak was not why. Fixing it moved calibration 7× and the stopping point 3 → 23 rounds, and the ranking not at all. The likely remainder: this signal is a sum of many weak pairwise interactions, which explicit crosses hold one-per-parameter and trees need a leaf per conjunction for. Untested.
  • L2 measured zero. Implemented, swept over six orders of magnitude, and it only ever cost AUC. The overfitting it would have cured was already removed by cutting advertiser cardinality from 400 to 60. The first sweep was also useless — too small a range to move the weights at all, which is its own lesson.
  • A scale bug that looked like a modelling result. Normalising the category embedding to unit length while the advertiser term had norm √32 let advertiser dominate the ad vector — 0.06 AUC, invisible until the magnitudes were compared.
